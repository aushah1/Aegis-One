How a grade is calculated

  1. Look. Aegis One records what your browser did on a site in the last 7 days (trackers, cookies, your cookie choice) and checks public lists (data breaches, data brokers, U.S. government lists) and, when it's been read, the site's privacy policy.
  2. Add points. Each rule in the table adds points for what was found. A company counts once, and only if it showed up on at least half of your visits, so reloading a page or a different ad winning the auction doesn't change the grade. Anything unknown adds 0.
  3. Cap and letter. The total is capped at 100 and turned into a letter with the key above.
  4. Range. Unknown lines set a range: the worst the score could be if they turned out badly. Today only the privacy policy can be unknown (up to 15 points).
  5. Label. Fully checked when every line has an answer, Partly checked when something is unknown, with the share of the grade's 115 possible points that has an answer. While Aegis is reading a site's privacy policy, it says Reading the privacy policy instead.

Each site's own numbers are under "How this grade was calculated" on the Sites tab. Fully checked doesn't mean safe: what companies do with data on their own servers stays unknown.

Observed

Connections your browser actually made while you were on a site. Aegis One sees these directly.

Disclosed

What a company says in its own privacy policy, like whether it sells or shares your information.

Unknown

What happens after data reaches a company's servers. Companies can pass it along server to server, out of any browser's view.

Foreign-adversary countries follow the U.S. Commerce Department list in 15 CFR § 7.4. A flag means a tracker belongs to a company based there, not that your data was sent to a government.

Your browsing and your questions to Aegis stay on this computer. Aegis One never sends anything about what you visit. Its tracker list is built in; it downloads only whole public lists (data breaches, data brokers) that are the same for everyone, Aegis's AI model only when you click Download Aegis's brain, and, when policy reading is on, a site's own privacy policy page from that same site, without your cookies. It saves site names and cookie names only (never full web addresses or what's inside a cookie), and deletes history older than 7 days on its own. Daily totals (numbers only, no site names) are kept for 90 days so you can see your week and month.

Built on what's new

    Aegis's brain. Ask Aegis answers your questions from what Aegis One saw, with a source tag on every fact, and reads privacy policies for "sells" and "shares", quoting the exact sentence. It runs on your own computer, so your browsing never leaves it.

    In shared/askc.js, shared/policy.js, shared/gemma.js · Gemma releases

    Runs Gemma 4 inside Chrome on the graphics chip (WebGPU), so the in-browser version of Aegis One needs no other app: first words in about a second.

  3. RegTrack study University of British Columbia, NDSS MADWeb, February 2026

    Measured 743 websites from 8 locations: accepting all cookies raised tracking from 48.5 to 56.2 domains per site from Ohio. Aegis quotes it, with page numbers, when cookies come up.

    In shared/regtrack-facts.json, content/regtrack-line.js · Paper

  4. Pentagon Section 1260H list U.S. Department of War, June 8, 2026

    The update that first lists Alibaba and Baidu as Chinese military companies. Aegis names the company, the list and its date when a page loads one of their services.

    In shared/flagged-companies.json, background/alerts.js · Federal Register

    603 companies that buy and sell personal information. Downloaded whole every day; trackers owned by a registered broker are marked and count in the grade.
